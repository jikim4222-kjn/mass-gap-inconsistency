# Proof

This file presents the logical structure of the reductio argument underlying
the inconsistency theorem.  
No additional assumptions are introduced beyond A3, A4, F1, and F2.

---

## Assumptions

Assume the following:

- **A3 (Single Vacuum):**  
  The Yang–Mills theory admits a unique Poincaré–invariant vacuum state,
  which serves as a complete and self-contained reference for all physical quantities.

- **A4 (Intrinsic Four–Dimensional Mass Gap):**  
  The mass gap is generated entirely within this single four–dimensional vacuum,
  without reference to additional layers, sectors, or external structures.

- **F1 (Vacuum Energy Observation):**  
  Vacuum energy is accessed through relative measurements; only differences
  between configurations are physically observable, while absolute contributions
  are scheme-dependent.

- **F2 (Interlayer Interpretation of Gaps):**  
  A mass gap may arise naturally as a difference between layers or sectors,
  without being intrinsic to a single layer.

---

## Step 1 — Tension between A3 and F1

Under **A3**, the unique vacuum is assumed to provide a complete and self-contained
reference state for all physical quantities.

However, **F1** indicates that vacuum energy is not accessed as an absolute quantity
internal to a single description, but only through relative measurements.
This places structural tension on the assumption that a single vacuum can function
as a fully self-contained reference for all physically meaningful quantities.

At this stage, no contradiction is asserted; only a tension is identified.

---

## Step 2 — Tension between A4 and F2

Under **A4**, the mass gap must be an intrinsic property of a single four–dimensional
vacuum.

By **F2**, however, a mass gap can arise as a difference between layers or sectors.
If such interpretations are admissible, then the mass gap is no longer intrinsic
to a single vacuum, contradicting **A4**.

Conversely, if all interlayer interpretations are excluded in order to preserve **A4**,
then the difference-based character of vacuum energy described in **F1** cannot be
naturally accommodated.

---

## Conclusion

The assumptions **A3** and **A4** cannot simultaneously hold in the presence of
**F1** and **F2**.

Therefore,

\[
A3 \;\land\; A4 \;\land\; F1 \;\land\; F2 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \bot
\]

This completes the reductio argument.
