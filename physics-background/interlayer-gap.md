# Interlayer Interpretation of Mass Gaps

This note provides physical intuition for interpreting mass gaps
as difference-based phenomena.
It is not used in the logical argument of the inconsistency note.

---

## Mass Gaps as Relative Structures

In many areas of physics, an effective gap arises not as an intrinsic
property of a single layer, but as a difference between layers,
sectors, or phases.

Examples include:
- energy gaps between bands or phases,
- effective masses emerging from collective behavior,
- universal responses insensitive to microscopic details.

Such gap structures do not require the introduction of new dynamics;
they arise from how descriptions are related.

---

## Relevance to Yang–Mills Theory

From a physical perspective, it is therefore natural to ask whether
the mass gap in Yang–Mills theory could be understood as a relative
or interlayer phenomenon.

The inconsistency note does not assume that this interpretation is
correct.
It only notes that excluding such interpretations a priori imposes
a strong structural restriction on the formulation of the problem.
